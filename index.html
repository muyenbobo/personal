Where I'm coming from

My path into tech started with Python, then grew into full-stack web development — JavaScript, APIs, authentication systems, and the everyday plumbing that makes an application actually work. Coursework has since pulled in Java and object-oriented programming, computer architecture, discrete structures, and differential equations alongside it, so I'm building the theory and the practice side by side rather than picking one over the other. I like building things that are practical: tools people open more than once — and lately, tools I've also written automated tests for.

Two things shape how I build. Human-computer interaction pushes me to think about who's using a thing and why an interface should feel obvious rather than clever. Operations research trains me to see a problem as a set of constraints and trade-offs before I touch a keyboard. Together they're the part of my work that doesn't show up in a stack list but changes how I approach everything else.

Outside of coursework, I take part in hackathons and side projects to keep building against real deadlines and real teams — most recently NextStep Hacks 2026, where I worked with a small international team on an environmental data platform. I also cook seriously in my spare time — enough that friends treat me as the resident expert whenever meat pie comes up.
